**Reading:** switching PVGIS 6 to the older c-Si module coefficients removes most of the gap; the 2025 module performance coefficients are therefore the main identified contributor. Switching reflectivity off changes output only slightly, and `temperature_model=None` leaves the output **identical**, so we cannot tell from outside whether that parameter is applied. The remaining difference after the module switch is **not attributed**: candidates include spectral correction (PVGIS 6 reports an energy-weighted spectral factor > 1; PVGIS 5.3 does not report whether it applies one), temperature/wind inputs and low-irradiance handling. We state this as unexplained, not as a conclusion.

## 3. Are the 26 PVGIS 5.3 zero hours really missing data?

- Computed hourly-average sun elevation in those hours: min 10.2°, median 30.5°, max 60.6° — far above the 5° threshold and the ~0.3° accuracy of the algorithm.
- KNMI Lelystad measured irradiance in those hours: min 38.9, median 322.2 W/m² (0 hours with 0); for comparison, median on ordinary producing daylight hours = 222.2 W/m².
- ERA5 temperature in the same PVGIS 5.3 records present and non-zero: True → only the satellite-derived fields are zeroed.
- PVGIS 6 reports null in 26 of 26 hours.

Threshold sensitivity. Column 2 uses the hourly-mean elevation (Stage 1 screening). Column 3 counts records with power, POA and reported sun height all 0, using the computed elevation at the **sampling instant HH:11**, the moment the PVGIS 5.3 values refer to:

| Threshold ° | Zero-power hours (hourly mean) | Zeroed records (elevation at HH:11) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 877 | 374 |
| 1 | 656 | 137 |
| 2 | 502 | 26 |
| 5 | 149 | 26 |
| 10 | 26 | 25 |
| 15 | 25 | 22 |

**Reading:** with the sampling-instant elevation, the zeroed-record count is 26 at 2° and 26 at 5° (stable), 25 at 10°. Below 2° it rises (137 at 1°, 374 at 0°): those are near-horizon samples where PVGIS's terrain-horizon model and refraction conventions can legitimately report a sun height of 0, so they are **not** evidence of missing data. A first version of this review used the hourly-mean elevation at low thresholds and over-counted for that reason; the sampling-instant criterion is the correct one.
